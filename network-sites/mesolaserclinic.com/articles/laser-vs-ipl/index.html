Laser vs IPL: What Is the Difference and Which Should You Choose?

July 31, 2026

Medically reviewed by Sarah Hitchcox, RN, BSN

Walk into any med spa and you will hear “laser” used for almost every light-based treatment. In reality, many of those treatments use IPL, which is not a laser at all. The two technologies overlap, but they behave differently, cost differently, and suit different problems. Here is the honest comparison.

The core difference: one wavelength vs many

A laser produces a single, precise wavelength of light. That wavelength is chosen to be absorbed by one specific target, such as the melanin in a hair follicle or the hemoglobin in a broken capillary. Because all the energy arrives at one wavelength, a laser can deliver strong, focused treatment to its target with less effect on everything around it.

IPL, short for intense pulsed light, is a bright flash lamp. It releases a broad band of many wavelengths at once, filtered to a chosen range. Some of that light reaches the intended target. The rest is absorbed by other structures in the skin. IPL is best understood as a wide net, while a laser is a spear.

Hair removal: laser usually wins

Both can reduce hair, but lasers are the stronger tool. A laser’s single wavelength can be matched to the follicle and driven deep enough to disable it. IPL’s scattered wavelengths penetrate less deeply and deliver less energy to the follicle, so IPL hair removal typically needs more sessions and produces less durable reduction.

IPL is also riskier on darker skin tones, because its broad spectrum includes wavelengths that surface pigment absorbs strongly. Most experienced providers treat deeper skin tones with an Nd:YAG laser and avoid IPL for those clients entirely.

Home-use “IPL laser” devices are much weaker than clinic machines. They can slow regrowth with consistent use, but they do not match professional results.

Sun damage, redness, and tone: IPL shines

Where IPL earns its keep is the classic “photofacial.” Because it emits many wavelengths, one IPL session can simultaneously target brown spots, diffuse redness, and small visible vessels across the whole face. For generalized sun damage and rosacea-type redness, IPL is often the more efficient and more affordable choice, usually done as a series of three or more sessions.

Lasers still win for specific, stubborn targets: a single dark sun spot, a distinct vessel, or deeper pigment problems respond better to a wavelength chosen for that exact job.

Comfort, downtime, and cost

Both treatments feel like quick snaps of heat, and both usually cause short-lived redness. IPL sessions often cost less per visit than comparable laser treatments, but may require more visits for the same end point, so compare full-course pricing rather than single-session pricing.

How to decide

  • Want lasting hair reduction? Choose a true laser, and insist on Nd:YAG if you have a deeper skin tone.
  • Want overall brightening of sun damage and redness? IPL is a reasonable first choice.
  • Have one specific spot, scar, or vessel? A targeted laser is usually better.
  • Not sure? Book a consultation and ask which device the clinic recommends and why. A good provider can explain the physics in plain language.

The bottom line

Laser and IPL are cousins, not twins. Lasers concentrate energy on one target and dominate hair removal and precise corrections. IPL spreads gentler energy across many targets and excels at overall tone. The right answer depends on your goal, your skin tone, and the skill of the person holding the handpiece.
