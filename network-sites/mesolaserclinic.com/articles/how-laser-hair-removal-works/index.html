How Laser Hair Removal Works, and How Many Sessions You Need

July 31, 2026

Medically reviewed by Sarah Hitchcox, RN, BSN

Laser hair removal is one of the most requested treatments in aesthetic medicine. It is also one of the most misunderstood. This guide explains what the laser actually does, why you need more than one session, and what a realistic timeline looks like.

The basic idea: light that targets pigment

A hair removal laser sends a focused beam of light into the skin. The pigment in the hair shaft, called melanin, absorbs that light and converts it to heat. The heat travels down the hair and damages the follicle, the small structure under the skin that grows the hair. A damaged follicle grows hair back thinner and slower, or not at all.

This is why the classic ideal candidate has dark hair. Dark hair absorbs more light, so more heat reaches the follicle. Very light blonde, gray, red, and white hairs contain little or no dark pigment, so most lasers cannot treat them well.

Why one session is never enough

Every hair on your body cycles through three phases:

  • Anagen: the active growth phase, when the hair is connected to the follicle
  • Catagen: a short transition phase
  • Telogen: a resting phase before the hair sheds

The laser only works on hairs in the anagen phase, because that is the only time the hair shaft is attached to the follicle and can conduct heat into it. At any moment, only a portion of your hairs are in anagen. On the face it may be a larger share, on the body often less.

Each session can only treat the hairs that happen to be in the growth phase that day. So treatments are spaced out, usually four to six weeks apart for the face and six to eight weeks for the body, to catch new groups of hairs as they enter anagen.

How many sessions most people need

Most clinics quote six to eight sessions for meaningful, lasting reduction, and that matches what published studies show. Some people see excellent results in fewer sessions. Others, especially those with hormonal conditions such as PCOS, may need more sessions plus occasional maintenance visits.

Be cautious of any clinic that promises permanent, total hair removal in two or three visits. The honest term used in medical literature is “permanent hair reduction.” Most people keep a large majority of the reduction long term, but a few fine hairs can return, and a yearly touch-up is common.

What a session feels like

Most people describe the sensation as a quick rubber band snap with warmth. Modern machines cool the skin during treatment, which helps a lot. Sessions are quick: an upper lip may take five minutes, while full legs can take closer to an hour.

Aftercare basics

  • Avoid direct sun and tanning beds before and after treatment, since tanned skin raises the risk of burns and pigment changes.
  • Skip waxing and plucking between sessions. Those remove the hair shaft the laser needs as its target. Shaving is fine.
  • Expect mild redness for a few hours. Blistering or lasting pain is not normal, and you should call your provider if it happens.

The bottom line

Laser hair removal works by heating pigment in the hair to damage the follicle. Because it only affects hairs in their growth phase, plan on six to eight sessions spaced several weeks apart. A qualified provider will assess your hair color, skin tone, and medical history before quoting you a plan.
