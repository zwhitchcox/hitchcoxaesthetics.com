22 Uses for Coconut Oil

Medically reviewed by Sarah Hitchcox, RN, BSN

One jar, many jobs. This list has been a reader favorite for years, so here is the refreshed, nurse-reviewed version. A quick rule before the list: coconut oil clogs pores on many faces, so keep the skincare uses below the neck unless your skin has proven otherwise. Details in our coconut oil guide.

Skin

  1. Body moisturizer. Best on damp skin after a shower.
  2. Makeup remover. Melts waterproof mascara. Rinse, then cleanse.
  3. Cuticle softener. Massage a dab into ragged cuticles.
  4. Heel balm. Apply thickly at night, add socks.
  5. Elbow and knee softener. The rough spots drink it up.
  6. Body scrub base. Mix with sugar for a shower scrub.
  7. Massage oil. Warms and spreads beautifully.
  8. Shaving alternative. A thin layer works for legs.
  9. After-sun softener. For after exposure only. It gives no sun protection, ever.
  10. Tattoo aftercare softener. Once healed and only if your artist agrees.

Hair

  1. Pre-wash deep conditioner. Apply to lengths an hour before shampooing.
  2. Split-end tamer. A tiny amount on the last inch of dry hair.
  3. Frizz smoother. Less is more. Half a pea for most hair.
  4. Beard conditioner. Softens coarse beard hair.

Kitchen

  1. Medium-heat cooking fat. Fine for sauteing and baking.
  2. Butter swap in baking. Use it solid, measure one to one.
  3. Cast iron seasoning. A thin wipe keeps pans happy.
  4. Smoothie addition. A spoonful adds richness. It is still a saturated fat, so moderation applies.

Household

  1. Wood conditioner. Buff into cutting boards and wooden spoons.
  2. Leather polish. Test a hidden spot first.
  3. Squeaky hinge fix. A dab works in a pinch.
  4. Sticker residue remover. Mix with baking soda, rub, wipe clean.

Two honest warnings

First, coconut oil is not medicine. It will not cure acne, eczema, or infections, and claims that it does outrun the evidence. Second, patch test before any new skin use, and stop if you see new bumps or irritation. A skin problem that keeps coming back deserves a clinician, not a bigger jar.
