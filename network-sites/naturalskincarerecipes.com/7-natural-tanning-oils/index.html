7 Natural Tanning Oils People Use, and What They Really Do

Medically reviewed by Sarah Hitchcox, RN, BSN

Natural tanning oils have been summer folklore for generations. This page has long been one of the most-read on this site, so here is the updated, nurse-reviewed version: what these oils are, what they actually do, and the safety truth that has to come first.

The seven classics

  1. Coconut oil. The most popular. It moisturizes well and gives skin a glossy sheen in the sun.
  2. Olive oil. An old beach staple. Rich and occlusive, and it attracts sand as effectively as it softens skin.
  3. Carrot seed oil. Loved for the subtle bronze tint its carotenoids lend the skin.
  4. Cocoa butter. Technically a butter. Deeply softening, with that classic summer smell.
  5. Avocado oil. Full of fatty acids, absorbs better than most heavy oils.
  6. Sesame oil. A traditional massage oil that leaves a light protective-feeling film.
  7. Raspberry seed oil. Famous online for a claimed high SPF. That claim traces to one misread study and has not held up under testing.

What these oils really do

All seven are decent moisturizers. They soften skin, add shine, and can feel soothing after a day outside. That is the honest list of benefits, and it ends there.

What they do not do is protect you. Measured sun protection for natural oils sits in the low single digits, roughly SPF 1 to 7 at best. That is not partial protection worth having. It is close to nothing, delivered with a shine that encourages you to stay in the sun longer.

A tan is skin damage

As a nurse-reviewed site, we will say this plainly. A tan is not stored health. It is your skin producing pigment in response to DNA damage that has already occurred. Tanning oils speed that exposure without blocking UV. Over the years, that shows up as wrinkles, dark spots, and a higher risk of skin cancer, including melanoma.

Sunscreen comes first

If you enjoy these oils, use them as after-sun moisturizers, not as sun companions. Before you go out, apply a broad-spectrum sunscreen, SPF 30 or higher, and reapply every two hours. If you want the bronzed look, a modern self-tanner delivers it with zero UV damage. We cover the full reasoning in Sunscreen Comes First: What DIY Cannot Do.

And if you spent past summers slicked in oil, keep an eye on your skin. Any spot that is new, changing, or refusing to heal deserves a professional look. Fading the dark spots and rough texture that follow is work for sun damage treatment in Knoxville or a dermatologist.
