Sunscreen Comes First: What DIY Cannot Do

Medically reviewed by Sarah Hitchcox, RN, BSN

This site is full of things you can make at home. This article is about the one thing you must not: sunscreen. If you take a single idea from all our guides, take this one. No natural recipe protects your skin from the sun, and sun protection matters more than every other skincare step combined.

Why sun protection is step one

Ultraviolet light is the main outside cause of skin aging. Wrinkles, dark spots, rough texture, and broken capillaries trace back to UV exposure far more than to any missed serum. Many of the lines that send people looking for Botox in Knoxville started as sun damage years earlier. UV is also the main preventable cause of skin cancer, including melanoma. A perfect routine without sun protection is a leaky boat with polished decks.

Why DIY sunscreen fails

Recipes for homemade sunscreen circulate every summer. They usually mix coconut oil, shea butter, and zinc oxide powder. They fail for reasons no careful cook can overcome.

First, the numbers are not there. Studies of popular natural oils measure their sun protection factor in the low single digits. Coconut oil comes in around SPF 1 to 7 at best. Raspberry seed oil claims of SPF 30 or more come from a misread study and have not held up.

Second, protection depends on an even, stable film of UV filter across the skin. Commercial sunscreens are engineered so zinc particles stay uniformly suspended, then verified with standardized SPF testing on human subjects. In a kitchen mix, zinc clumps and settles. Your arm might get SPF 20 in one patch and SPF 2 next to it, and you cannot tell which is which. Published tests of DIY recipes have found real-world protection far below what the recipe promised.

Third, there is no water resistance, no photostability testing, and no way to know when the batch has separated.

A tan is not a base, it is a signal

One more myth needs retiring: the “healthy base tan”. A tan is your skin’s response to DNA damage that has already happened. Tanning oils, natural or not, speed exposure without meaningful protection. If you love the look of a tan, self-tanner gives it without the damage.

What natural oils actually offer

Oils like coconut, jojoba, and argan are fine skincare ingredients. They soften skin and support the moisture barrier, and they can soothe skin after sun exposure. Used before sun exposure they do almost nothing protective, and the sheen can even encourage longer time in the sun. Enjoy them as moisturizers, not as armor.

How to do sun protection right

  • Use a broad-spectrum sunscreen, SPF 30 or higher, every morning.
  • If you prefer the natural end of the aisle, choose a tested mineral sunscreen with zinc oxide. That is the honest version of the DIY recipe.
  • Apply enough: about a nickel-sized amount for the face alone.
  • Reapply every two hours in the sun, sooner after swimming or sweating.
  • Add shade, hats, and sunglasses. Fabric is the most natural sunscreen ever invented.

When to get skin checked

See a clinician about any spot that is new, changing, bleeding, or refusing to heal. Annual skin checks are a good habit, especially if you are fair-skinned or spent years tanning.

The bottom line

Make your masks and scrubs at home. Buy your sunscreen from a company that tests it. That split, honest about what DIY can and cannot do, is the whole philosophy of this site.
