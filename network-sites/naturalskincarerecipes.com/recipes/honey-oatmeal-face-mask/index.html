Honey and Oatmeal Face Mask: A Gentle Classic

Medically reviewed by Sarah Hitchcox, RN, BSN

If there is one home skincare recipe worth keeping, it is this one. Honey and oatmeal are gentle, cheap, and easy to find. Together they make a soothing mask that most skin types tolerate well.

Why these two ingredients

Honey is a humectant. It draws water toward the skin and helps it feel soft instead of tight. Raw honey also has mild antibacterial properties, which is one reason it has been used on skin for centuries.

Oatmeal is best known for calming irritation. Colloidal oatmeal, which is simply oats ground to a fine powder, is an FDA-recognized skin protectant. It shows up in many drugstore products for eczema and itchy skin for good reason.

What you need

  • 1 tablespoon plain rolled oats, or 1 tablespoon colloidal oatmeal
  • 1 tablespoon raw or regular honey
  • A splash of warm water

Skip flavored oatmeal packets. They contain sugar and additives you do not want on your face.

How to make it

  1. If you are using rolled oats, grind them in a clean coffee grinder or blender until they look like flour.
  2. Stir the oat powder and honey together in a small bowl.
  3. Add warm water a few drops at a time until you have a spreadable paste.

How to use it

  1. Wash your face and pat it mostly dry.
  2. Spread a thin layer over your face. Avoid your eyes and lips.
  3. Leave it on for 10 to 15 minutes.
  4. Rinse with lukewarm water and pat dry.
  5. Follow with your usual moisturizer.

Once or twice a week is plenty. More is not better. Make a fresh batch each time, because the mix has no preservatives and will not keep.

Patch test first

Always test a new recipe before you put it on your face. Apply a small amount to the inside of your forearm. Wait 24 hours. If you see redness, itching, or bumps, do not use it.

Who should skip this mask

  • Anyone with a honey or pollen allergy.
  • Anyone with an oat allergy or celiac-related sensitivity to contaminated oats.
  • Babies and young children. Do not use honey on broken skin in infants.
  • Anyone with open wounds, active cold sores, or a flaring skin condition.

What this mask can and cannot do

This mask can leave your skin feeling calm, soft, and comfortable. That is a real benefit, and for many people it is enough.

It will not clear acne, fade dark spots, or change wrinkles. No kitchen recipe will. If you are fighting a persistent skin problem, a short visit with a medical professional will save you months of trial and error. See a dermatologist for medical concerns, or a Knoxville med spa for cosmetic ones.

The bottom line

A honey and oatmeal mask is one of the safest recipes in home skincare. Patch test, keep the mask away from your eyes, and enjoy it for what it is: a gentle, pleasant treat for your skin.
